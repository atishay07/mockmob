A fictional archive asked volunteers to describe old photographs. A description was stored as confirmed only when a dated label or an accompanying document identified its subject. Memories offered by visitors were recorded separately as possible identifications. Several visitors initially disliked this distinction because they were confident about their recollections. The archivist explained that preserving a recollection did not require presenting it as established fact. Later, a letter confirmed one visitor's identification, and that record was updated. The system allowed information to be reconsidered without discarding the history of how it had first been reported.
