The station clock had stopped at ten past four. Each morning, travellers looked up, checked their watches and continued towards the platform. The tea seller never corrected the clock; it was beyond his reach. One afternoon, a child asked whether the trains had stopped too. He pointed to a train sliding past the far platform. Its windows caught the sun one after another. The child watched until it disappeared, then looked back at the clock. For the first time that day, the tea seller noticed the contrast himself: the hands stood still above a place where almost everything was moving.
