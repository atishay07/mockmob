A fictional neighbourhood reading room introduced a reservation system for its single discussion table. Groups could book thirty-minute slots, but no group could book consecutive slots. Unreserved time remained available to any visitor. After a month, more groups had used the table, although some regular visitors complained that they could no longer hold long meetings there. The committee kept the reservation rule and offered a separate room for meetings lasting more than thirty minutes. Its decision aimed to share access to a scarce facility while accommodating a different need elsewhere. The report did not suggest that short meetings were inherently better than long ones.
