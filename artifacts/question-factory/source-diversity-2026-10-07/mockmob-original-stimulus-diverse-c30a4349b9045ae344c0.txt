In a fictional exhibition, visitors received either a printed map or a map containing the same information with larger lettering. Volunteers recorded how often each group asked for directions. Those receiving the larger lettering asked less often. The organisers noted that volunteers had handed out the maps on different days, when different galleries were open. They proposed a second trial in which both versions would be distributed on the same day. Meanwhile, both maps remained available. The first trial provided a reason to investigate readability, but it could not separate the effect of lettering from differences between the days.
