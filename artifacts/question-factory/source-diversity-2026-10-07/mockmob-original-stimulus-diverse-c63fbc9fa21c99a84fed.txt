Amina practised a speech until she could recite every sentence. On the morning of the competition, the organiser shortened each speaker's time. She tried to speak faster, but the words became hard to follow. Her teacher asked which idea she most wanted the audience to remember. Amina removed an anecdote and two examples, retaining the central argument and one supporting example. She finished within the new limit. Afterwards, she missed the anecdote she had enjoyed writing, yet she could explain why it had been removed. The shorter speech preserved her purpose even though it did not preserve every sentence.
