Rain had washed the chalk game from the lane. The children arrived with fresh chalk and began drawing again. One child remembered the old squares as longer; another insisted that the turning square had been on the left. They argued until a passing neighbour asked whether the new game had to reproduce the old one. After a moment, they drew a wider turning square in the middle. Their feet soon covered the fresh lines with pale dust. From her doorway, the neighbour could no longer tell which marks belonged to yesterday and which belonged to the game they had just invented.
