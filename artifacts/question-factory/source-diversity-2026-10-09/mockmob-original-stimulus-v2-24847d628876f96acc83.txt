A good map is less a picture of land than a promise about what a traveller will find. Its symbols are therefore choices, since the maker had to decide which roads, streams and hamlets deserved a place on a sheet of paper. Consider a route marked with a dotted line: the dots tell the reader that the path exists but has not been checked, so the traveller carries extra food. The lesson is that a map earns trust only when its uncertainty is visible, because a reader who cannot tell surveyed ground from guesswork will be misled.
