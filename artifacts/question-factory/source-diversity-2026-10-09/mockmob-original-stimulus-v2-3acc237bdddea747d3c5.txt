A visitor approaching the salt works at Corrin's Reach meets a low wall of grey stone, its top furred with pale crust left by the evaporating brine. Beyond the wall, a gravel yard stretches toward a row of shallow pans, where the surface gleams like hammered tin. At the far end of the yard stands a timber shed, its doors propped open to let the damp air out of the drying room. Inside, the shed is dim and sharp with the smell of brine, and the workers' benches are lined with sacks of coarse white grains.
