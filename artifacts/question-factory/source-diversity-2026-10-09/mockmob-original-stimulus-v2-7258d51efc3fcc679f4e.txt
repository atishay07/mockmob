The members of the Hollin Reading Circle commonly assumed that the quietest person at each meeting cared least about the books they discussed. However, the notebook kept by Mrs Anand, who rarely spoke, contained summaries more careful than any the group had produced. She listened to every argument and wrote down the points that others had made in passing, because she preferred to weigh ideas privately rather than offer her own in a rush. Silence, it turns out, reveals a different kind of attention, so the group has learned to ask quiet members for their notes instead of guessing at their interest.
