The harbour town of Lowmere suffered from litter that gulls tore open and scattered along the quay. The council answered by installing twelve large steel bins with heavy lids, hoping that more capacity would end the problem. The lids were so heavy that residents left their bags on top of the bins, where gulls could reach them more easily. The lesson for Lowmere was that capacity alone cannot change a habit, and that a solution must make the right action easier rather than merely larger.
