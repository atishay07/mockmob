The village of Ostrey shared its grazing meadow among every household, and each family kept a few sheep on the common grass. A newcomer named Hale bought the estate beside the meadow and raised a stone wall that enclosed the common, claiming the grass was his by right. The villagers refused to accept the loss of their grazing, so they tore down sections of the wall in the dark and carried the stones to the square. The council granted them a footpath across the estate so the sheep could reach the meadow by that narrow route.
