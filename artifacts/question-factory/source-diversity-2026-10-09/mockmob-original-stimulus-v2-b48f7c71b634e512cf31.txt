The Ashdown school magazine is produced in two different ways, one in which an editor assigns every article and another in which writers pitch their own ideas. The assigned method has the advantage of producing a balanced issue, since the editor can make sure that every section receives a piece of similar length. Its weakness, however, is that the editor's own tastes quietly decide what the school reads, and talented writers who were never asked to contribute are left unheard. The pitched method, though messier, gives writers a voice, so a mix may work best, with the editor assigning the gaps and writers filling the rest.
