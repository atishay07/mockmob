Public libraries ought to lend tools as well as books, because a shared hammer serves a whole street more fully than a single shelf of manuals ever could. Admittedly, a library that lends saws and drills must spend money on repairs and must trust borrowers with objects that can cause harm. Yet a drill that sits unused in a private garage is a quiet waste, and the occasional repair bill is modest beside the many small jobs a shared drill makes possible. The library's purpose is to put useful things into common hands, so lending tools extends that purpose rather than departing from it.
