# Feature adoption: what top products do, and what MockMob changes (17 Oct 2026)

**Origin:**
- Owner ask F18: last year students didn't use 90% of the features, so learn how top edtech platforms lead students to features and make paid value felt.
- Research subagent, 17 Oct about 1:30 am IST.
- Evidence tags: **[A]** a number exists (often self-reported); **[B]** a primary description; **[C]** inference.

## Why "simple" failed last year

A simple look doesn't tell a student what to do next. Students who must recognise "I need feature X now" and go find it mostly don't.

- **Khan Academy:** the first Khanmigo "did not change student learning as much as many of us hoped", because students had to know when to ask. The rebuild put the tutor inside the practice problem. [B]
- **Superhuman:** optional tooltips and checklists had no measurable impact. Unskippable setup panels and teaching the single best path roughly doubled the results. [A]
- **Chameleon (vendor benchmark):** tours the user starts beat timed popups 2-3x. Tours over 5 steps lose half their users. Only about 23% of users ever open a checklist. [A]

**Rule:** the product decides the next action, each feature appears at the moment it is useful, and the student sees a result at once.

## Ranked changes for MockMob

1. **One loop: Find, Fix, Keep.**
   - Mistake Lab finds where marks went. Repair fixes one mistake. Revise keeps it.
   - Every mock ends in Find, and the way forward is Fix, then Keep.
   - Top-level places: Today, Practice, Mocks, Mistakes, Colleges. Everything else is reached from a moment, not a menu. [B/C]
2. **Today is one hero card, "Up next".**
   - One big action with a one-line reason ("You lost 11 marks to changed answers, so 6 minutes of Repair"), plus at most two quiet items.
   - "Choose something else" is a small link.
   - Then a done-for-today state that previews tomorrow.
   - Sources: Duolingo path, Khan queue, Speak. [B]
3. **Result screen in three layers, never a stack.** [B/C]
   - Score, the marks ledger, and one sentence naming the biggest leak.
   - One primary button, "Fix your biggest leak".
   - One collapsed "Full analysis", with SWOT and time analysis as tabs inside it.
4. **Repair sits on the lost-mark row itself**, like Duolingo's "Explain My Answer" on the answer pop-up.
   - The first Repair is free as a sample.
   - Then the cost sits beside the marks at stake: "1 credit. This cost you 4 marks." [B]
5. **Receipts that make paid value visible:**
   - after Check it held: "Fixed. This trap cost you 4 marks in Mock 3. Held 1 of 1";
   - after the next mock: "Of the 6 mistakes you repaired, 5 did not come back";
   - a weekly receipt on Today.

   Count only what can be honestly tied to a repair; never claim a cause. [C]
6. **Value before setup in the first session:** a short check, a real result, one real mistake explained free. Then a two-field setup with a stated payoff. Source: Duolingo's delayed sign-up, about +20% DAUs, self-reported. [A]
7. **The first week is a chained path, and Today is that path:**
   - Day 1: a check, then Repair one mistake free.
   - Day 2: Revise comes due.
   - Day 3-4: smart practice.
   - Day 5-7: the first full mock.

   Road to May starts with step 1 already counted, with a reason (the endowed-progress effect, Nunes and Drèze, 34% vs 19%). Never a separate checklist widget. [A/C]
8. **Introduce each feature at its moment, never in a tour:**
   - Revise after the first Repair;
   - the Lab after the first mock;
   - Ask Mobi inside a question review;
   - Colleges and Compass after the first full mock, once there is a score;
   - NTA mode as a Road to May milestone ("dress rehearsal");
   - the group feature after a 7-day run. [A/C]
9. **Ask Mobi lives in the work** (a prefilled "Ask Mobi about this question"), not as a destination students must remember. [B, Khan]
10. **Empty states that teach:** one line on what will appear here, a button to the next action, and a sample row. [B, NN/g]
11. **Plain, marks-priced mistake names:** changed a right answer, rushed, never reached, trap, concept gap. Drop "SWOT" as a student-facing heading. [B, Embibe]
12. **One small daily habit with a clear definition** (one Repair or one Revise round). The student chooses the length and can opt out. No shaming. [B]
13. **Few, specific notifications,** each tied to one real item ("3 mistakes due"). [A, Duolingo]
14. **"You improved because" cards on the next result:** "You beat your last mock by 14 marks. 9 came from mistakes you repaired." [C]

## Stop doing

- every analysis on the result screen at once;
- a home screen that lists all features and makes the student choose;
- tours, modals or popups on first open;
- relying on tooltips or an optional checklist;
- Ask Mobi as a tab;
- naming features before the student has what they apply to (Compass before a score);
- jargon headings (SWOT);
- stacked prompts after a result;
- mass or shaming notifications;
- locking basics to look simple (no evidence it helps).

## Measure

At our scale, use before-and-after cohorts plus 5-8 student sessions per change.
- **Per feature:** saw, tried, used a second time.
- **Week-one activation:** first mock, plus first Repair, plus a return on day 2 or 3.
- Read last year's usage data first.
- Ask: "How would you feel if you could no longer use MockMob?" (Superhuman's 40% benchmark is a correlation only).
