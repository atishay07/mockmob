## 0. What "taste" means here (owner, 18 Oct about 7:50 pm, D92; this section governs the rest)
Taste is NOT only his preferences. It is three sources combined, and a critic or builder uses all three:
1. **Independent UI/UX expertise:** the model's own knowledge of what good design is. That covers hierarchy and the one primary action; affordance and discoverability; Fitts's law and 44-48 dp targets; Gestalt grouping; type scale and rhythm; motion that explains state (ease-out, 150-350 ms, interruptible); platform conventions (Material, iOS); accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA); and first-run learnability. Also how the best products solve the same problem.
2. **His research brain:** docs/research/premium-ux-ui-2026-10 (AGENT_RULES, the 12-part anti-slop test, the evidence IDs). Where it disagrees with older rules, the research wins.
3. **His records:** the sections below, the ledger's "Never again" and "Keep" lists, and his own words.

**When they disagree:** his explicit rejections and praise win, then the research brain, then general principles. Every improvement names which of the three it rests on.

**This loop is an experiment (D93), not a permanent stage.** Its usefulness is measured blind tonight. Keep improvements that are clearly better; never trade a working flow for taste.

## 3. Taste checklist (a critic applies all 15 to every screen, at 390 and 1440, Night and Day, in use)

1. **Unique:** with the logo covered, it is still MockMob, not a template. Name its one ownable detail.
2. **Premium reference:** it holds up side by side with HIGH, Revolut, Fampay or Apple. Glass is on the chrome and physical; the art is rich, not flat vector stand-ins.
3. **One leading action:** within one glance the student knows the single next tap, and it leads on to the next step.
4. **Self-explaining first run:** a first-time user understands every control without being told. Gestures are shown and coached the first time, sliders look draggable, and buttons look pressable.
5. **No form:** no stacked fields or segmented rows. Settings and plans are guided, interactive steps with a live readout.
6. **Rich, not cut:** against the last approved version, nothing good is lost (mascot moments, full analysis, rich cards). Items on the Keep list are untouched.
7. **Calm structure:** clutter is solved by grouping and layering, not by deleting. Nothing has to be decoded; every dot, bar or indicator carries real information (M-005).
8. **Mobi alive and adaptive:** Mobi reacts to what the student just did, is sized to the free space, and is never cramped, asleep or puzzling.
9. **Copy:** bold, specific, persuasive marketing English. Zero disclaimers, filler or build jargon. Every number is real.
10. **Consistency:** one label per action across the screen, and every count agrees.
11. **Layout safety:** a real gap under the status bar, balanced top and bottom, nothing under the bottom bar. A clear Back button, not only a small icon. Targets of 44 px or more.
12. **Motion:** every state change moves with a purpose; it is springy, interruptible and at least as good as the version before. One indicator slides, never two boxes. A haptic on meaningful moments (app).
13. **Night subtle, Day equal:** no neon, glow, gradient text, pulsing pills or kickers. Night is as quiet as it was before R2.
14. **Instant:** no needless confirm sheets. A tap answers at once, and nothing stays stuck loading.
15. **Visible:** his ask is obviously changed, and the note says exactly where to look. The screen is neither over-gamified nor sparse.

## 4. How he gives feedback, so critics write the same way

- He uses the real thing (phone, emulator, live site), **screen by screen**, and names the screen first ("Coming on the Practice section...").
- **Verdict first:** "huge regression", "perfect now", "good direction". Then what to keep, then what is wrong.
- **What he physically sees, with no abstractions:** "almost into the notification bar", "first box arrives before the second box", "only gray boxes going and going".
- **He compares to apps he knows** (HIGH, Revolut, Fampay, Apple, Duolingo, Tinder, Opal, "most apps do this") **and to earlier versions** ("it used to be something else").
- **He takes the student's view:** "If I see it from the student's point of view..."
- **He offers ideas and leaves the call to us:** "You be the judge", "random ideas". "Small things... but they matter."

**Critic note format** (one per finding, ranked most visible first):

`[Surface > screen, viewport, theme] VERDICT: KEEP / REFINE / REGRESSION. Like: <what to keep>. See: <what is physically wrong>. Fails: <checklist #> ("his quote"). Fix: <one concrete change with values>. Like in: <reference app>. Where to look: <route or step>.`

Example: `[App > Today > swipe stack, 390 Night] REFINE. Like: the card art and the lead-card idea. See: a first-time user gets a card with no cue; the left and right meanings are invisible until after a swipe. Fails: #4, #3 ("it won't know what cards actually do"). Fix: on the first visit the top card nudges about 24 px left and right with "Skip" and "Make it my lead" labels revealed under it. During a drag, a label grows with the offset and a haptic fires at the commit threshold. Keep the Skip and Do-this-instead buttons as the visible path. Like in: Tinder's first-run hint, plus taste-picking card stacks. Where to look: Today, first launch after onboarding.`
