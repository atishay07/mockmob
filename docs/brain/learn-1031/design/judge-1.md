# Learn concepts, judge 1 (owner lens)

Judged 31 Oct 2026 by a judging subagent. Nothing was built or changed. Inputs: OWNER-FEEDBACK-2026-10-31-LEARN (LK1-LK14), the maps R1-R9 (read in full for R7 sections 1, 4-12 and R8 parts 1.3-5; the rest skimmed for facts), OWNER-TASTE-PROFILE, the ledger's Never again and Keep lists, AGENT_RULES, the walk screenshots (app Learn door, realdeck Study 390, web Read 1440 Day), and the three concepts A-composer, B-chapters and C-two-lanes, read in full. CTO decisions B1-B8 treated as fixed.

Lens: he opens Learn on his phone as a 16-year-old would, screen by screen, and asks "where do I tap, what happens, is this MockMob or a template, is it confusing".

## Verdict

**B wins as the skeleton; graft A's tangible parts onto it.** B is the only concept where his own LK10 sentence is the literal route (subject loop, chapter map, chapter page "lined up", time, Start, mix) with two levels and nothing to decode, and it reuses the subjects loop he called "perfect". Its weakness is that its home is a long list and its chapter page readout is a sentence, which risks "basic" and "lines of text". A has the best answers to exactly those weaknesses (live piles, the card flying into a dated Revise pile, one name for recap, an honest answers-up Explore feed). C is the most thorough on evidence and metrics but the most crowded and the most jargon-heavy, and it touches two Keep surfaces (Today, Start sheet) more than needed.

## Fatal flaws, plainly

**A**
1. Learn opens on our choice, not his. The composer is pre-filled with a chapter and a length; picking your own chapter is a sheet away (5 taps) while a full chapter map sits below it: two chapter pickers on one page. That is the LK9 and LK10 order inverted.
2. "Study [Admission of a Partner v] for [10 minutes v]" is fill-in-the-blanks with dropdowns. A teen needs "Tap any word to change it" to use it (Never again 3, taste 4).
3. Free picks 10 and sees "Start 7 minutes"; Free Learn questions spend the day's included set and then credits, so learning feels metered.
4. Suggestions load the composer instead of starting: a tap of friction (D53).

**B**
1. The Learn home is a 14-17 row text list (ring, state word, count, meta line). On a phone that is the "lines and lines of text" and "basic" risk (LK11, D56) unless rendered with real richness.
2. Two names for the recap job ("Quick recap", "Flip through these 12") and two for Study ("Cards only", "Study these"): the Read vs Last look confusion comes back in new words.
3. The Explore feed rates cards, writes to Revise and blocks the swipe until rated: it is a duplicate of the Learn run with friction added.
4. Questions free and unlimited inside Learn is a pricing change against B8's one included set a day; it must go to him as a decision, not ship silently.
5. The chapter page stacks a length row and a mix row of chips over a sentence: close to a form.

**C**
1. At 390 the home stacks the top bar, the Practice tab row and a sticky glass lane switch before any content, then three "For you" cards each with its own length chips: many equal controls (AS2), the opposite of calm.
2. Things to decode: "How sure: Sure / Likely / A first guess", short and long pips for cards and questions, "kept" and "back soon" (Never again: strips a student has to decode).
3. Text density: per-ingredient lined-up rows with minutes, a boxed three-column "What's here" with sub-lines, explanatory lane copy (LK11, D185).
4. Adds a Learn tile to Today and a second step inside the Start sheet, two Keep surfaces he praised.
5. Silently schedules the linked card as Missed after a wrong answer, and changes the Free allowance to count per question.

## Best ideas to graft into B

From A:
1. **Live piles as the readout** on B's chapter page: a Key cards pile, a questions pile and (at 20 min) a lesson pile that count up and down as the length changes; tap a pile to leave it out ("Left out", tilted and dimmed). Replaces B's text sentence and makes the chooser a tangible object, not a form (D47, N1, taste 1, 5).
2. **Got it flies into a "Revise pile"** stamped with the server's real return date; Missed slides to a "Soon" slot; the pile fans open at the session end with return dates grouped. Replaces B's flight into a progress pip (R8: the card moves to its next-review slot).
3. **One name, "Quick recap", everywhere**: the chooser choice and "Recap these" in Find and Cards. Drop "Flip through". Use "Study all 46" (whole chapter, rounds of 20) as Study's single label.
4. **Explore feed answers-up, no rating, "Keep this card"** to add to Revise, with the one-line difference stated once. Removes B's duplicate-mode risk and the rate-before-swipe friction.
5. **The chooser as one component with a glass-sheet skin** opened from Today, the Start sheet row and the result's "The line you needed", pre-filled with that chapter; the same piles and Start key.
6. **A Learn card type in Today's swipe stack** (right swipe makes it Up next, D94) and the session-end lead rule (missed 3 or more: go again on those; else next chapter).
7. **Mobi reacting to the chosen length** and once to the tally; real seconds per item recorded from day one and the estimate recalibrated per student.
8. **"Your own" sheet that says what the chapter fills** and offers to carry on into the next chapter (H1, no cap).

From C:
1. **Three suggestions always of three different kinds**, each with one evidence line, with a test that asserts it for new, returning, Free and offline fixtures (LK11, G12).
2. **A named metric and guardrail** (time to first card; sessions ending over 125 percent of the chosen time) for R37 and RUB instrumentation, which B lacks.
3. **First-session coach lines**, once in a student's life: "Say it in your head first.", "Missed: back tomorrow. Got it and Easy space it out.", "Pick one. If you miss it, you see the NCERT line you needed." (D90, G23).
4. **"One at a time" works on any filter** in Find and Cards (flip through "Missed lately", not only Must-know), plus a "Show answers" switch on the list.
5. **Chapter-page states** "Nothing is due here" and "Every card is on its schedule, next due Thursday" so a finished chapter still offers a real 10 minutes.
6. **Result screen's second quiet link**, "10 minutes on this chapter", opening the chooser sheet with the chapter set.

Keep from B itself: the subjects loop as the switcher, the part switch at the Part 2 point, suggestion keys that start at once, honest shrink with "Add the next chapter", the 1440 sticky "In this chapter" shelf, the rating row pinned above the gesture bar, the sheet code in the print footer, and the folded "Marks open" sort.
