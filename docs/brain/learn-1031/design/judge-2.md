## Learning science: what is honest and what is not

1. **A linked question placed straight after its card tests memory over a few seconds.** That is close to re-reading, not retrieval. All three concepts do it: A ("a linked question placed straight after the card it tests"), B ("preferring a question linked to a card just shown") and C ("pulled forward to follow its card"). Fix: put the linked question 3 to 6 items after its card. That is a short spacing inside the session, and the card is still fresh enough for the line-you-needed loop to make sense. This keeps the honest benefit of mixing without the fluency illusion R8 warns about.
2. **B places an open mistake right after its linked card "so the line comes before the retry".** This shows the answer before the retest. Recall becomes recognition, and the end screen's "2 of 3 fixed" is inflated, which breaks AS9 (no pseudo-evidence). Fix: retry the mistake first and show the line only after a miss. A's Meera walk-through already does this: her own wrong question comes first, then its card.
3. **Read rows that show the start of the answer kill self-testing.** A shows the first truncated line and B shows two lines. C folds the answer behind a tap, with a "Show answers" switch. C is right for a revision library.
4. **Feed retrieval.** A's Explore feed shows cards answer-up with no rating, which is passive. C's "Tap to see NCERT's line" plus Keep keeps a retrieval attempt and changes no schedule without a visible key, as D94's spirit asks. Take C's version.
5. **Spacing honesty.** A's Revise pile with the server's real `next.due_at` stamped on the card, and a session end that fans the pile with dates grouped, is the clearest truthful picture of spacing in all three concepts (R31, AS10). Take it.
6. **Recap of what was never learned.** A and B both offer a new student a "Quick recap" of a chapter they have not studied. A recap of unseen material is reading, not recap. C's cold start ("Find your level: 6 Accountancy questions") is the first diagnostic action the MockMob overlay rule asks for. Use that for students with no history.
7. **Interleaving evidence** covers problem types in maths, not a blend of flashcards and MCQs (R8 2.3). No copy may claim learning gains. A's line "Each question follows the card it tests" is fine only when the questions really are linked.

## Feasibility on the mapped engines

All three need the same new server work:
- a server-graded answer route that sends no key and writes `user_question_progress`;
- the inverted card-to-question index;
- a chapter filter on `dueCards`;
- the Must-know overlay.

That work is real, and R3 confirms none of it exists. The concepts differ in what else they assume:
- **A (1):** "Uses 6 of today's 10 free questions" assumes the Free allowance counts per question. Today it is per 10-question set (`daily_practice`, R3 line 42), and A does not flag this. A also charges credits per question inside a mixed run, which is a new charging path.
- **B (2):** keeps questions free, on Explore's footing (R3 confirms Explore is free and unlimited). That removes all credit plumbing. **But it is a pricing decision beyond B8, so it needs his yes.** B flags it.
- **C (1):** flags the per-question counter. It also adds a For-you engine with a Dart port, a Today tile, a second step inside the praised Start sheet, and automatic Missed ratings.

## Fatal flaws

**A**
- The hero is a sentence-shaped form with tap-the-word affordances that need coaching. That is a first-run risk for exactly the audience in G23.
- Composers on both home and the chapter page duplicate the entry point.
- The home is pre-filled by the system. "Every word is yours to change" is control by editing our pick, which is weaker than his LK9.
- The Free per-question allowance does not exist and is not flagged.
- The Explore feed is passive (answers up).

**B**
- Last look's job keeps two names ("Quick recap" and "Flip through"), which half-repeats LK7.
- Showing the line before retrying a mistake inflates "fixed".
- Free questions in Learn is an unapproved pricing change.
- A map-first home risks his "basic list" verdict unless the art and the live readout carry it.
- A card flying into a progress pip mixes progress with scheduling.

**C**
- Two lanes plus three For-you cards, each with its own chips, is choice overload at 390.
- Pip lengths encode the item kind, which is a strip to decode.
- "How sure" copy.
- Automatic Missed ratings the student never gave.
- It touches two praised surfaces: a new Today tile and a second step inside the Start sheet.
