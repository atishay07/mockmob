## Abstract [I]

Three before/after comparisons address a subscription trial, a ride selection flow and a villa booking screen. Their common denominator is *decision quality*: clarify a commitment, reduce unpredictable prices, and answer practical and emotional objections. The creator repeatedly says one mockup 'performs dramatically better,' but neither a controlled experiment nor a real launch metric is established in the transcript. They should be treated as narrated comparative design cases, not independently verified A/B test outcomes.

## Case 1: A trial that explains rather than pressures, 00:36–05:19 [T007]

**Source-side A [T]:** A $19/month gaming subscription pitch with decorative space hero art, generic benefit bullets, and a CTA that combines subscribing with a seven-day trial.

**Source-side B [T]:** A trial explanation with a clear three-step schedule: day of activation, reminder on day five, and first billing on day seven. The CTA focuses on starting a trial; supportive detail claims a concrete two-tap start. The proposed preview uses actual game characters instead of a generic image.

**Creator reasoning [T]:** Reduce perceived commitment and uncertainty; show concrete steps, user benefits, a candid reminder, specific effort estimate and identifiable product content.

**Independent critique [I]:** The timeline must reflect actual billing/notification logic. A 'reminder sent' assertion is forbidden without a functioning event-delivery system and appropriate permission. 'Start my free trial' cannot hide the fact that a subscription renews; price and auto-renewal must remain visible. The two-tap promise must be true for the user's actual journey, including authentication and payment. Real product preview is stronger evidence of what is sold than irrelevant artwork.

**MockMob application [I]:** If offering a free trial, expose a real sequence of feature access, trial deadline and subsequent billing. The plan should say exactly what students receive and what happens at the end. Show actual test modes and examples rather than abstract 'unlock unlimited potential' copy. A/B-test comprehension and cancellation satisfaction alongside conversion.

## Case 2: Quoted fares vs uncertainty, 06:55–09:37 [T008]

**Source-side A [T]:** Three ride types with fare *ranges* (narrated examples include $13–17 and $17–22), leaving users to estimate final cost.

**Source-side B [T]:** A single presented price per ride, estimated arrival such as '2 minutes away', a destination card on the map, and a conspicuous cheaper label.

**Creator reasoning [T]:** People can compare definite values more easily than several uncertain ranges, and arrival information changes the perceived decision from pure expense to convenience. The creator explicitly contrasts labels such as cheaper and the anchoring effect of the highest possible fare.

**Independent critique [I]:** A definite *quote* is beneficial if the product can honor it. Presenting an uncertain estimate as a guaranteed price is deceptive. If supply, surge pricing, taxes or fees vary, represent a realistic range with a plain-language rationale and a final confirmation step. The 'cheaper' badge needs a clear comparison baseline. This case does not justify hiding meaningful uncertainty.

**Circuit application [I]:** In event listings, distinguish fixed costs from 'from ₹x', approved times from tentative timings and guaranteed seats from waitlists. Where an offer is definite, prefer one clear value; where uncertain, say so.

## Case 3: From booking form to specific experience, 10:37–13:57 [T009]

**Source-side A [T]:** Small villa thumbnail, factual headline, 4.82 rating, €89/night, dates and 'reserve'.

**Source-side B [T]:** Much larger property photography; trust badges (for example guest favorite), photo count, specific emotionally engaging description, price comparison (€129 crossed out against €89), explicit weekday/date/night count, CTA incorporating €445 total and a free-cancellation detail.

**Creator reasoning [T]:** Show what the user is getting, support emotional imagination, remove date/math work and answer concerns about actual final cost and changes of plans.

**Independent critique [I]:** Large product imagery helps only when accurate, optimized and not crowding out task completion; the crossed-out reference price must be a real, valid comparison, not invented anchoring. The total must include all mandatory taxes/fees or be accurately qualified. 'Free cancellation' must match policy. A badge must be a verified designation. Creator's assertion about relative booking performance cannot be upgraded to a causal claim.

**MockMob application [I]:** Exam-mode descriptions should preview a *real* experience and expected length, number of questions and scoring conventions. CTAs should indicate the action: 'Start 45-minute Accountancy mock' instead of 'Explore'. Clearly expose prerequisites, payment, credit usage and whether a test can be paused/resumed.

## Cross-case lessons [I]

1. Say what happens next: exact steps, duration, cost, cancellation, or state transitions, based on actual behavior.
2. Visual proof beats abstract decorations when users need to know what is being offered.
3. The true total is more helpful than an apparently small fragment of cost.
4. If uncertainty exists, name and contextualize it rather than pretending it is a fixed value.
5. Frame benefits in the user's task language, not only commercial slogans.
6. Claims of 'A/B winners' should remain attributed until the actual trial methods and results are available.
