## Feeling curve (written before the devices)

1. Recognition: a late-night student sees one question they can answer now.
2. Relief: a small companion helps an illustrative mistake become one next step.
3. Control: their subject choices connect to published DU rules and cutoffs.
4. Trust: creator accounts, dated facts and explicit product limits can be checked.
5. Resolve: five questions tonight feels achievable; the companion settles beside the closing action.

Engineered peak: the Mistake Lab changes a tangle of lost marks into one chapter to practise. It keeps the largest interactive reading surface. No extra scroll spacer or compulsory pin.

Tell-someone sentence: "It's the site where a little volt scout follows the mistake from your mock to what you do next."

Signature move: Pip's night shift. The same character greets beside the demo, thinks beside the replay, points beside the published cutoff surface, and settles at the close. Section-local transform/opacity travel makes a continuous narrative without a screen-covering fixed follower. Mobile keeps a reserved, small character seat. Reduced motion renders static poses.

## Grammar and devices

Grammar: guided working journey, a deliberate refinement of the existing site. Semantic sections, jump navigation and live sample controls stay usable independently. No full-screen film, scroll hijacking, moving study data or photographic endorsements.

The eight stock grammars lose because: filmic one-shot forbids jump navigation; editorial delays the playable sample; live surface forbids the existing marketing hierarchy; continuous world replaces the functioning page; typographic poster loses the demo; catalog turns study into browsing; split stage imposes a binary argument; rhythmic cutlist outruns reading. This custom grammar names its bans rather than borrowing their labels.

| Beat | Device | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | Existing layered hero + operable drill | Immediate proof through a solved question |
| Relief | Replay + section-local pose change | Explain the next move, the single peak |
| Control | Real filters and historical numbers | Visitor changes a meaningful input |
| Trust | Quiet flow, sourced facts, native horizontal reel rail | Verify rather than wait for a spectacle |
| Resolve | Static close, settled character | One visible action remains |

Runtime: existing CSS and Motion only. Native CSS scroll timelines as progressive enhancement; complete static fallback. Character assets are transparent WebP, lazy except the greeting. No video, WebGL, Kie or paid API.

## Mascot creative brief, before generation

Pip is a pocket-sized signal scout, a CUET study companion. Warm, observant, a little cheeky; never a teacher who grades you or a student who endorses us. Its silhouette is a soft folded volt kite with one asymmetric short antenna, graphite visor, two warm cream eyes, short dark arms and two broad feet. One fold line gives it a recognisable paper origin without fragile detail. Palette: #D2F000 volt, #18241E graphite, #F2F4EC cream. Crisp editorial illustration with modest dimensional shading, no plastic toy gloss. The visor reads at 64px; no words baked into the image. At 11pm it says "one useful step" rather than "try harder". It occupies a small reserved seat on a 360px screen and costs kilobytes, not a new animation runtime.

Character sheet first. Then greeting, thinking, pointing, correct-answer celebration, idle and worried-but-encouraging cutouts derived from that sheet. Identity, proportions and palette must remain fixed. Inspect alpha and edges on light and night before publishing assets.
