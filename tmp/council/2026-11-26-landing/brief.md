# Council brief: what the MockMob phone home page should keep, cut, fold and in what order

## The product and the reader
MockMob (www.mockmob.in) is a mock-test and practice product for CUET UG 2027, India's university entrance exam. The
reader of the home page is a 16 or 17 year old on a mid-range Android phone, often new to computer-based tests and to
paying for apps. Every sign-up comes from a phone. Price: free to start; Pro is Rs 99 once, to 31 July 2027.

## What the owner said today (his words, dictated)
- "One piece of feedback I have got is that the website is a lot cluttered so I personally don't know what to remove.
  From a frontend perspective I need to showcase all the features that we have and as much as possible but maybe
  that's not the right approach."
- "You can do anything to the website right now ... add anything, remove anything, or majorly rearrange things here
  and there, grouping them or shifting them."
- "Honestly, the clutter is not very prominent on the desktop. I think people on phones might be facing this a bit more."
- "Like this Daily 5 and Sunday mock, I really don't like its implementation right now, and even this DU compass, it
  was previously almost perfect. Now it's a bit [off]."
- "Even our pricing guide maybe needs a bit of a revamp on the phone. We have replaced icons from features, but I'm
  not sure if that was the right approach. It could be the right approach, but I have again received a conflicting
  opinion on this. I personally like this one more."
- "Build something which is not AI slop." This is the final pass before he locks the page.

## Things he has said before that still stand
- He judges on his phone. Earlier full restructures of the page were rejected as regressions; refinements of the live
  page were accepted. He liked: the hero film, the "one place" frame with real product screens worked by a cursor, the
  Mistake Lab story, the CUET 2026 check, Ask Mobi.
- Size and place things by importance; no walls of text; back every claim; a section must say what the feature is.
- The headline differentiator is unlimited mocks on Pro (rivals give 10 to 30 a subject).
- The desktop page is fine. Changes may be phone-only.

## The questions (answer every one)
Q1. On the PHONE page, which sections or blocks leave, which merge or fold behind an opener, and what is the final
    order of what stays? Give the order as a numbered list in DETAIL. Say roughly how many screens long it should be.
Q2. Hero on the phone: what stays under the film? (The six-feature grid, the "Rs 0.20 a mock" line, the calculator
    link, and the "works best on a laptop" note above the headline are each in question.)
Q3. The Daily Five and Sunday Mock block: what form should it take on the home page? The owner dislikes the current
    one. Known earlier forms: a calendar-page card he approved on 16 Nov; an admit-card game he found too much; three
    explainer pictures plus an example demo, which took 1,584 px; today's pair of cards with openers. It may also move,
    shrink to a strip, become a tab of "One place", or leave the home page (it has its own pages and a nav link).
Q4. Pricing on the phone: keep the six feature pictures, replace them with something else, or drop them? What is the
    order of the parts, and what goes?
Q5. DU Compass on the phone: what to trim so it reads cleanly (the ten-row list, the five things under it)?

Answer shape for this brief: one block a question, each with POSITION, WHY (2 or 3 points), RISKS (1 point). End with
one line, BIGGEST SINGLE CHANGE: the one change that would do most for a phone reader.
